# Weeks 3–4 — commissioning, trust, and the one-month review

By now four articles are live and the pipeline is proven twice (a port and a full edit). Weeks 3–4 turn that into an operating magazine: pick the next pieces from the ratings, build the trust page, and review the whole thing against reality.

## W3-1 — Commission the next two deep-dives

Pull from the ratings, choosing for **audience spread**, not just score (the wave-2 ratings explicitly spread the five winners across notebooks for maximal coverage).

Candidates, all already rated:

| Idea | Wave | Score | Why it earns a slot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 "The Matter logo is a conformance claim, not a compatibility promise" | 2 | **25/25** | The only perfect score; the hook *is* its integrity lesson |
| 6.1 "The 82% do-nothing baseline" | 2 | 24/25 | Single reframing number; strongest chart in wave 2 |
| 22.2 "Money on three clocks" | 2 | 24/25 | Personal-finance reach; broadest non-technical audience |
| X.1 "Certified vs working" | 2 | 24/25 | Cross-cutting flagship: lab → living room |
| 5.1 "The Ledger That Blending Can't Fool" | 5 | 24/25 | Best wave-5 idea; origin/verifiability |

**Rule:** commission only what can be written from the corpus **as-is** — the F criterion. Anything needing primary research waits.

**Acceptance:** two commissioned, drafted, and through the W2-2 checklist; ideally one shipped inside the window.

## W3-2 — Build the trust page

`24.1 "What the corpus admits it doesn't know"` (24/25) was cut from the wave-2 top five for a good reason: *readers don't choose gap audits — editors link them.* That makes it perfect for the **standard page**, not the shelf: a linked "how we show our work / what we don't know" section that every article can point to.

**Acceptance:** the standard page carries a real, evidence-grounded honesty section — not a slogan, an inventory of the limits.

## W3-3 — Measurement (privacy-light)

Decide what "working" means, and watch only that. Candidate signals: unique readers, return readers, time-on-article, inbound links, and — most on-thesis — **whether the evidence layer is opened at all**.

**Constraint:** no invasive tracking; prefer aggregate, self-hosted or none. This is a decision (**D9**), default: start with nothing, add only if a question actually needs answering.

**Acceptance:** a one-line definition of success recorded; if instrumentation is added, it is aggregate and minimal.

## W3-4 — The one-month review

A written memo that answers, honestly:

1. Did the standard survive contact with readers? (the six-question test, on real articles)
2. Was the evidence layer used, or is it ceremony? (feeds the standard's own falsifier)
3. Did the "claimed vs accountable number" thesis fit every article, or only some?
4. What did porting/editing reveal about the dataframe-elephant intake?
5. Is the two-week cadence real, or aspirational?

**Acceptance:** memo written; the standard and the board updated to match; falsified assumptions named, not buried.

## W3-5 — Backlog triage

Move the remaining rated ideas into a visible queue with scores and wave provenance (see [07-decisions-and-backlog.md](07-decisions-and-backlog.md)). Nothing is commissioned without a rating and a corpus check.

## What "done" looks like at one month

- 4–6 finished deep-dives on the shelf, each passing the standard;
- one standard page carrying the promise and the limits;
- a written intake (Article Ideas → repo), a checklist, and a cadence;
- a review memo that revises the plan where reality disagreed with it.
