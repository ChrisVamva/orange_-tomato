# RAG or a maintained knowledge wiki: which one earns its upkeep?

![The Orange Tomato mascot — a tomato-headed robot in cracked dark armor — standing beside a shelf of source boxes, its chest screen showing the article title; the wall behind is sprayed with SOURCES SYNTHESIS UPKEEP TRACE STALE.](/images/rag-vs-maintained-wiki-lead.jpg)

The mistake was small and confident. Our maintained wiki said the Kestrel S lantern weighed 410 grams and was "the heavier pick". Then the manufacturer's erratum arrived: 410 grams had included the retail box. The bare lantern was 365 grams. Nothing about the light had changed — but a sentence in our synthesis, and the tradeoff built on it, was now wrong. The wiki had not lied. It had remembered too well.

That is the choice this piece tests. A persistent, LLM-maintained wiki promises to carry synthesis forward between questions, so you pay once and reuse. Retrieval-augmented generation promises the opposite discipline: go back to the source documents at query time and synthesize fresh. For which research tasks does the extra curation actually improve answers — and when does it just preserve errors?

We ran the comparison on a small, versioned corpus we built for the test — fictional Bluefin lantern specs, so no real product is at stake — asking the same questions both ways across four tasks: a one-time lookup, a cross-source synthesis, a newly added contradiction, and a correction. The answer is task-by-task, not a winner. The wiki was faster where synthesis repeats, equivalent where lookup is one-off, and silently stale where sources changed until someone paid the update cost. What mattered was not the architecture alone but update discipline and question mix.

Start with what each path is. In the retrieval path, each question triggers a search over versioned source documents, then a fresh synthesis from whatever is retrieved. In the wiki path, synthesis is written ahead of time into a maintained page; each question is answered from that page, which is only as current as its last update. A useful mental model is a cache: caches help on hits and hurt on invalidation. The test below is essentially a cache test with citations attached.

The corpus was deliberately small: five base documents (three spec sheets, a charging guide, one field note), a wiki synthesis page built from them in about 25 minutes, then two dated changes — a lab retest and a weight erratum. Retrieval here was manual keyword search, not a production vector database, and one operator built both paths. That bounds everything that follows: this establishes the logic of the tradeoff, not production speeds or general rates.

Task one was a one-time lookup: what is the Bluefin L3 low-mode runtime? Retrieval found the L3 spec sheet (24 hours, 25°C, full charge, dated 2026-09-10). The wiki gave the same figure from its prebuilt section. Tie, with similar per-question effort. The wiki's 25-minute build cost sat in the background, unamortized — paying to synthesize what you ask once buys little.

Task two was cross-source synthesis: which lanterns meet USB-C charging *and* more than 20 hours of low-mode runtime, and what are the tradeoffs? Retrieval needed four to five document opens — L3 (24 hours, USB-C, 320 g), Kestrel S (22 hours, USB-C, heavier housing), L2 (18 hours, Micro-USB, excluded) — plus the charging guide and the warmth field note, then a fresh synthesis. The wiki answered from one section that already held that synthesis, with the same tradeoff and the same caveat about running warm on high while charging. Same substance, less per-question work. This is where a maintained page earns its keep: when the same synthesis is reused, pre-paying it pays off. The caveat is symmetrical: had the synthesis question been novel — not already in the wiki — the advantage would have vanished.

Then the corpus changed, and the tie broke.

The contradiction arrived as a dated lab retest: one L3 unit on a different battery lot ran 19 hours, not 24, with no root cause established. Asked again with explicit instruction to surface conflicts, the retrieval path returned both figures with their dates and lots and stated the conflict as unresolved. The un-updated wiki still said 24 hours. It was not wrong about what its sources had said in September. It was stale about what the shelf now held in October — and it said so confidently, with citations that stopped at its last update. Updating it to match the retrieval substance took about 15 minutes here: a conflict note, both citations, and the lot caveat.

![The Orange Tomato mascot in profile holding two dated sheets apart, its chest screen showing the article title; the wall behind is sprayed with CONFLICT DATE LOT CHECK FRESH.](/images/rag-vs-maintained-wiki-contradiction.png)

The correction cut closer to reuse-as-evidence. The 410-gram figure the wiki had used to justify "the heavier pick" was superseded by the 365-gram erratum. Retrieval, seeing both the old spec and the dated erratum, preferred the erratum and softened the tradeoff (still heavier than the 320-gram L3, but by 45 grams, not 90). The un-updated wiki kept repeating the 410-gram tradeoff as settled synthesis. The error had not just persisted; it had been promoted from a spec line into a conclusion. Fixing it took about five minutes plus a wording check here — cheap at this scale, and exactly the kind of ripple check that gets skipped at larger scales.

Traceability followed the same pattern. At the start, both paths cited sources a reader could inspect. After the changes, retrieval citations carried the new dates forward (retest date, erratum date), while stale wiki citations pointed back at pre-change synthesis. A wiki *can* match that traceability — per-claim source links plus an update log would do it — but only if someone maintains it. The mechanism does not maintain itself.

So which questions earn a wiki? The pattern in this run is narrow but consistent: repeated, stable synthesis amortizes curation; churn and correction punish it. If your questions re-ask the same cross-source comparisons over settled sources, a maintained page saves repeated synthesis at a measurable upkeep price. If your sources contradict, correct, or turn over quickly — or your questions keep being novel — re-reading the shelf each time keeps freshness and traceability that a cache cannot give you for free.

Two larger limits stay with that sentence. First, the lead that motivated this test — a breakdown of an LLM-maintained wiki pattern associated with Andrej Karpathy, described as acknowledging error propagation and source-scale limits — has not yet been inspected in this run. Treat it here as a proposal that shaped the test design, not as evidence for any claim. Second, production realities on both sides are untested here: vector retrieval can miss the very addition you need through chunking or ranking, and wiki maintenance at hundreds of pages with multiple editors has failure modes this five-page corpus cannot show. Our timings are one operator's wall clock, not rates.

The practical consequence is therefore conditional: for a researcher or small team, audit your question mix before you audit tools. If the same syntheses recur and your sources sit still, fund the notebook and its update log. If contradictions and corrections are the work — or novelty is — fund the re-read. Either way, keep the shelf versioned and dated, because both paths are only as checkable as the citations they preserve.

The best-supported answer from this run is not that one architecture wins. It is that persistence is a tradeoff with a maintenance bill attached: faster accumulated synthesis against source freshness, traceability, and the risk that a mistaken summary gets reused as the evidence for its own conclusion. The wiki that said 410 grams was not careless. It was un-updated. That distinction is the whole decision.

## Sources and method

Test corpus (synthetic, fictional Bluefin lanterns; no real product): five base documents (L3 spec 24h/USB-C/320g 2026-09-10; L2 spec 18h/Micro-USB/280g 2026-09-10; Kestrel S spec 22h/USB-C/410g v1 2026-09-12; charging guide 2026-09-10; warmth field note n=1 2026-09-20), wiki synthesis v1 (~25 min, 2026-09-22); changes: lab retest L3 19h lot B-27 n=1 2026-10-05; erratum Kestrel S 365g bare unit 2026-10-12. Full versioned record in the article run evidence file.

Method: same four verbatim questions to both paths (manual keyword retrieval proxy vs maintained-page read), un-updated wiki read first, then updated wiki timed. The retrieval proxy is not a vector RAG system; ranking/chunking untested. One operator, single trials, informal timing. No private data, no external sends.
